Before you play: layers of the same lift

Lift is the aerodynamic force perpendicular to the freestream. It comes from pressure and shear on the wing surface. Different “theories” are usually different descriptions of one flow—not competing religions.

Pilot (PHAK)

Faster upper flow → lower static pressure (Bernoulli). Wing also imparts downwash (Newton). PHAK: both operate. Symmetrical airfoils still lift at AOA.

Engineering

\(C_p = 1 - (V/V_\infty)^2\). Section \(C_L \approx 2\pi\alpha\) (thin airfoil, rad). 2-D: \(L' = \rho V_\infty\Gamma\) (Kutta–Joukowski). Finite wing: induced drag & downwash.

Why viscosity matters

Ideal inviscid flow doesn’t pick a unique circulation. A sharp trailing edge + viscosity enforces the Kutta condition, selecting \(\Gamma\) so flow leaves the TE smoothly.

Complete stack (one flow, many views)

Use this as the “graduate” summary card.

  1. Definition: Lift from integrated surface pressure & shear.
  2. Bernoulli / Cp: maps surface speed to pressure.
  3. Newton / momentum: far-field downwash & force balance on a control volume.
  4. Circulation: L′ = ρVΓ with Γ fixed by Kutta (2-D).
  5. 3-D: tip vortices, induced drag, span efficiency.
  6. Reality: viscosity, separation, compressibility, unsteady flow limit every simple model.
Myth: Equal transit time — air above and below must rejoin after equal times.
Truth: Upper flow is typically faster; parcels do not take equal time. Lift does not require that myth.
Myth: Bernoulli or Newton — pick one.
Truth: Complementary. PHAK: both operate when lift is produced.
Myth: Only the bottom “pushes up.”
Truth: Full pressure field; upper-surface suction is often large.

About / equations / limits

  • Bernoulli form shown is incompressible, steady, inviscid, along a streamline.
  • Thin-airfoil \(C_L \approx 2\pi\alpha\) (α in radians) is small-AOA theory for thin sections; stalls are cartooned.
  • Kutta–Joukowski is 2-D; real wings need finite-span corrections.
  • Canvas drawings are pedagogical—not wind-tunnel data.
